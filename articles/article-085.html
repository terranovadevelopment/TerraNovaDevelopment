The Real Cost of Healthcare: Why Medicare for All Is Cheaper for Everyone

Download Word document

Opponents of Medicare for All (MFA) often claim they’ll “pay more in income and employer taxes.” What they rarely acknowledge is that they already pay far more — just not through taxes. The current system hides its true cost behind premiums, deductibles, copays, and corporate overhead. When you add it all up, Americans are already funding a system that costs nearly twice as much as MFA would.

1. What Employers Pay Today

Under employer-provided insurance, companies shoulder the majority of premiums:

  • Average employer premium contribution (single coverage): $6,300–$7,600 per worker
  • Average employer premium contribution (family coverage): $20,000 per family
  • National total: $1.1–$1.3 trillion per year

That’s money that could otherwise go toward higher wages, hiring, or lower prices. It’s a hidden tax — paid privately instead of publicly — and it vanishes under MFA.

2. What Workers Pay Directly

Employees pay their share of premiums plus all out-of-pocket costs:

  • Employee premium share: $1,400 (single) to $6,800 (family) per year
  • Deductibles: $3,000–$6,000 (single) and $6,000–$12,000 (family)
  • Copays and coinsurance: hundreds to thousands more annually

Even insured families often skip care because they can’t afford these costs. Under MFA, premiums, deductibles, and copays disappear entirely.

3. The Indirect Costs

Beyond premiums and out-of-pocket expenses, the current system drains the economy through:

  • Administrative overhead: HR departments, brokers, compliance, billing, and claims management — $30–$50 billion annually
  • Private insurer overhead: 12–18% of revenue, compared to Medicare’s 2% — $150–$200 billion wasted
  • Lost productivity: workers delaying care or missing work due to untreated illness
  • Medical debt: $195 billion in unpaid bills, damaging credit and consumer spending

These indirect costs push the total burden to $9–$10 trillion per year — nearly half of U.S. GDP.

4. The Deflationary Bonus

Medicare for All doesn’t just replace private spending with public funding — it reduces total costs. When businesses no longer pay premiums and households no longer face medical debt, prices across the economy fall. Lower overhead and healthier workers mean:

  • Downward pressure on wages and prices (deflationary effect)
  • Increased consumer purchasing power
  • Reduced inflationary pressure on goods and services

Economists estimate MFA would cost around $5 trillion per year, but that spending replaces the $10 trillion we already pay — and it buys more care for everyone.

5. The Bottom Line

Under the current system:

  • You pay premiums, deductibles, copays, and taxes.
  • Your employer pays massive premiums and administrative costs.
  • You still risk bankruptcy if you get sick.

Under Medicare for All:

  • You pay through taxes, but less overall.
  • Your employer pays nothing for health insurance.
  • Everyone gets full coverage — no deductibles, no copays, no denials.

Medicare for All isn’t about ideology — it’s about arithmetic. We already fund the world’s most expensive healthcare system; MFA simply funds it more efficiently, transparently, and universally.

Editorial note: This is an advocacy article. Cost figures and projections are reproduced from the supplied text and have not been independently verified. Some figures may overlap or mix different cost categories. A Medicare for All system would replace premiums with public financing, and actual household and employer outcomes depend on its design.
