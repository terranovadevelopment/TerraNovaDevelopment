Medicare for All: Where the Legislation Stands

Article #057 · November 11, 2026

Download Article #057 (Word document)

Current Status

The Medicare for All Act was introduced in the 119th Congress in two companion measures: H.R. 3069, introduced by Representative Pramila Jayapal on April 29, 2025, and S. 1506, introduced by Senator Bernie Sanders the same day. Both were referred to committees.

As of the sources reviewed for this article, neither proposal had become law. Specific cosponsor totals and the latest hearing or vote activity should be checked against the live congressional records before publication or sharing.

What the Bills Propose

The proposals would establish a national Medicare-for-All health insurance program with broad coverage, including hospital and physician services, prescription drugs, mental-health care, dental and vision services, and long-term care. They would substantially change the role of private health insurance and reduce or eliminate many patient charges.

The details differ between the House and Senate texts. The bills also raise major questions about financing, provider payment, transition timing, and implementation.

What Happens Next

Committee consideration, amendments, and congressional votes would be required for either proposal to advance. Introduction alone does not mean that legislation is scheduled for a floor vote.

Supporters argue that a universal single-payer system could improve access and simplify administration. Critics question its financing, federal spending implications, and effects on providers and existing coverage arrangements.

Bottom Line

Medicare for All remains a significant legislative proposal and an active subject of national policy debate. Readers should distinguish what the bills would do if enacted from what Congress has actually approved.
